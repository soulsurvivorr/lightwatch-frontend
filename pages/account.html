About & support How LightWatch works, and how to get help

LightWatch is a crowd-sourced power-status tracker. Instead of waiting on official outage reports, people in your area flip a simple switch to confirm whether the light is currently on or off where they are — and the app rolls those individual reports up into a live status for the whole neighborhood.

The more people who check in for a location, the more reliable that location's status becomes. That's what the "Reliability" and "Source confidence" numbers on your Home page reflect: how consistently recent reports agree with each other.

Your contact info is masked everywhere it's displayed in the app, and chat messages are posted under an anonymous handle (like anon-glow-291) rather than your name — other users never see who you are.

Your city/town can only be changed once after signup, which keeps location-based status reports trustworthy for everyone else watching that area — see the note on the city edit card for details.

Only flip the light switch or post a status update if you can actually see the area right now — reports feed directly into what your neighbors see, and bad data makes LightWatch less useful for everyone.

In community chat, keep it local and practical: outage updates, generator/fuel tips, and questions for people nearby. Anything abusive or unrelated may be removed.

Status looks wrong for your area? Open community chat and ask — someone nearby can usually confirm within minutes. If notifications aren't arriving, check that they're enabled above and that your browser allows notifications for LightWatch.

For anything else, reach the team at support@lightwatch.gh.
