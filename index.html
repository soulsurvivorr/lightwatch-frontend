Know before you leave home

When power is unstable, a wrong guess costs time, transport money, and productivity. LightWatch gives you a live, community-driven signal so you can decide quickly whether to move, wait, charge, or reroute your day.

Your report affects real decisions

People use this feed to choose when to open shops, travel, study, or run critical tasks. Post only what you have personally confirmed. One careless report can mislead hundreds of people in minutes.

  • Confirm first, then report.
  • Accurate updates protect everyone from costly false moves.
  • Matching updates from your neighborhood raise confidence in each status.
  • Frequent participation turns uncertainty into fast, confident decisions across Kumasi.

Before you continue

  • Live but time-sensitive: Conditions can change quickly. Always combine LightWatch updates with your latest local check before making major decisions.
  • Responsibility first: False or outdated submissions reduce trust and can cause avoidable losses for others in your area.
  • Independent platform: LightWatch is community-run and not operated by ECG or any government agency.

    About & support How LightWatch works, and how to get help

    LightWatch is a crowd-sourced power-status tracker. Instead of waiting on official outage reports, people in your area flip a simple switch to confirm whether the light is currently on or off where they are — and the app rolls those individual reports up into a live status for the whole neighborhood.

    The more people who check in for a location, the more reliable that location's status becomes. That's what the "Reliability" and "Source confidence" numbers on your Home page reflect: how consistently recent reports agree with each other.

    Your contact info is masked everywhere it's displayed in the app, and chat messages are posted under an anonymous handle (like anon-glow-291) rather than your name — other users never see who you are.

    Your city/town can only be changed once after signup, which keeps location-based status reports trustworthy for everyone else watching that area — see the note on the city edit card for details.

    Only flip the light switch or post a status update if you can actually see the area right now — reports feed directly into what your neighbors see, and bad data makes LightWatch less useful for everyone.

    In community report, keep it local and practical: outage updates, generator/fuel tips, and questions for people nearby. Anything abusive or unrelated may be removed.

    Status looks wrong for your area? Open community report and ask — someone nearby can usually confirm within minutes. If notifications aren't arriving, check that they're enabled above and that your browser allows notifications for LightWatch.

    For anything else, reach the team at support@lightwatch.gh.
